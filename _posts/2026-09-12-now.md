### turning thirty

I feel I need to update my internal image of myself.
As a thirty yearold man, my role in society and in my communities is distinct from that of a young man in his late teens or early twenties.
I'm not sure what my new role is but I suspect my sense of age needs to adjust.
This subtle and ambiguous process will accelerate and clarify when I become a father.

### ruminating on the next few years

What the next few years will look like has been a question hanging over me for many months, years even.
As time passes the picture starts to look clearer.
More and more Z sees babies in person and on social media and more and more she says – playfully still – _I want a baby..._
And then she says she doesn't want one yet but I know and she knows that at some point pieces of an invisible puzzle will click into place and all of a sudden the fancy will precipitate into real consideration.
She says she has always known when the time came for taking the next step in her life.
It's true.
She's intuitive and decisive.
I'm not waiting for her like she jokes I am but both of us feel the growing proximity of parenthood.
And as it gets closer the pressure grows on us to settle other aspects of our lives.

### reading

I bought a few books last August when visiting Ireland and England and this summer I started reading them.
While flying to Montreal in July I read the little book of short stories by Shirley Jackson I bought at a thrift store in London and while in Montreal I started the little copy of _Investigations of a Dog_ by Franz Kafka I bought at the same shop.
In Bristol I bought a cheap copy of _Dubliners_ by James Joyce but ended up reading a digital copy on my phone during our visit of Indonesia.
When I was in Dublin I chatted with a guy roughly my age working at a little bookshop and I told him Cormac McCarthy was a favorite writer of mine and he recommended _The Sheltering Sky_ by Paul Bowles.
A few days later I came across a copy at a thriftshop and bought it.
Today, the day we are flying back home, I started reading a digital copy of it.

It's like a little game, reading books I own and moving them from my larger collection of unreads to my slowly growing row of reads.
I also justify owning copies of books I've read by intending to revisit them in impulsive bouts of reading.
I don't expect to read them all anew but instead to drop into them at arbitrary positions and read delicately in little pieces.

### working for our condo's strata

In addition to reading and playing fantasy football, I have been dedicating mental energies during my vacation in Indonesia to organizing our strata's efforts to add new bylaws.
We're trying to create visitor parking stalls and prohibit Airbnbs and ban smoking inside units but our property manager is not orchestrating these efforts.
I found motivation to catch up on all the different email threads and write several emails and set out a plan.
Our property manager is unwilling to request the city for documents related to our building because "that's not what [he does]" so I have gotten my first experience of communicating with the city bureaucracy.
It's frustrating and confusing as expected but luckily the staff has been responsive.

### distracting myself less

My numbed reaction to the unexpected passing away of a friend recently alerted me of how strained was my connection to my feelings.
Like anybody else I don't like feeling difficult feelings and also like many others I sometimes disconnect myself from myself to forego suffering.
Months ago I wrote about the chatter I habitually feed into my consciousness throughout the day in the form of podcasts and videos.
Writing about it brought about the usual benefit: I developed the observation into a coherent idea on top of which I could make further observations.
I noticed a pattern and by writing about it I distilled it into an abstraction which then freed my mind to zoom out and observe "higher level" patterns: ways in which that abstraction fit into greater patterns.
My next discovery has been to intuit a relationship between my impulse to consume external stimulus with my aversion for going to sleep and, likely, for drinking beer and eating in the evenings.

In bed, in the dark and quiet, thoughts and ideas and fears and observations and worries denied throughout the day their time in the spotlight push out onto center stage.
Finally, because they can find no hearing otherwise, they command attention.
All those difficult feelings neglected await treatment.
At no point am I conscious of this.
All I know is I don't want to go to bed.
I eat and drink and watch TV and delay sleep until it's so late I am likely to fall asleep quickly.
Once in bed I put an earbud in and listen to something funny and distracting until I sense sleep is ready to take me out of consciousness.

These habits make it obvious something is amiss.
But there is no arbiter – other than myself – responsible for observing and challenging them.
My wife and other loved ones can only say so much without embarrassing me or feeling like they are nagging me.
It's up to me to confront myself about how I'm treating myself.
But there's a conflict of interest because I'm the one benefiting from these unhealthy behaviors.
I'm the one asking to be treated this way.

So I'm pulling the stopper and letting all that's clogged in my subsconscious flow out.
While I cook my lunch I no longer put on earbuds and listen to podcasts.
Instead I play instrumental music aloud in the background and let my thoughts be the primary thing on my mind.
(I think the secondary stimulus of instrumental music softens the edge without compromising the activity.)
And now, instead of eating my lunch at the couch in front of the TV, I eat at the dinner table facing the patio.
In short, I've reduced external chatter so that I can process my internal chatter.
