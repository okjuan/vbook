### getting dismayed by corporate politics

Most the seven years I've worked at this megacorporation I've ignored "reorgs."
Who cares?
The guy seven levels above me is a different guy now.
So what?

So much, it turns out.
In the second half of September, my eyes were opened to the petty and political motivations that determine the destiny of corporate products.
No, it doesn't matter what customers want – unless enough of them want something that will move the bottom line.
What matters – if the righteous don't or can't intervene – is the retaining of power, the retaining of influence.
I'm reminded of the passage in _Bullshit Jobs_ in which David Graeber recounts the story of an employee who excitedly presented to upper management a substantial optimization of corporate process he had developed.
The VPs whose departments would benefit from the proposal reacted not with gratitude or excitement but anger.
What mattered most to them was maintaining as many underlings as possible, no matter how needless was their employment.

What I've learned these last few weeks is I must keep in constant contact with certain coworkers so that I might collect information about the fate of our team.
Rest in peace my naive and optimistic assumption that management will make sensible and earnest decisions in the interest of customers or for the vision of a worthy product.

### thinking more conservatively about the future

There was a time in the late 2010s and the very early 2020s when opportunities seemed endless to me.
Being a single young man moving to a new city on megacorporate dollar only amplified my feeling that a wealth of opportunities awaited outside my frontdoor if I felt so inclined to go out and engage them.
I lacked nothing so obvious.
And yet in my new daily life I was not so satisfied.
I worked everyday in an office at the edge of a forest where began a wasteland of freeways, strip malls, car dealerships, and corporate campuses.
Many other fortunate infant professionals like me arrived into similar circumstances and stepped gladly onto the conveyor belt of wealth accumulation and career progression.
These were nice people but I found myself disinclined to meld into this social milieu.

Seven years on I am much happier.
Although I work at the same company, life is much more my own.
I work in the comfort and convenience of my own home, at a desk windowside.
I deliver what is expected of me, at least, and retain the freedom to tend to personal and domestic tasks when they need tending.
I work on a project I chose to join more than four years ago because I was interested in it.
I work on the web frontend, despite the stigma the would claim it's not "real software engineering."

No doubt I've dabbled in audiobooks and investments, I've driven my career forward.
But after these same seven years I don't worry so much about keeping up with my peers.
I realize I don't care so much that my company isn't cool, I care more for contentment at work, good compensation, and stability, now and in the future.
All of a sudden I'm not so anxious about delving into a new world, a new company, a new experience.
I like what I have.
I want to stabilize it, sink stakes into the ground, and set up camp for a while.
I yearn now not for breadth, but depth.
I want to go deeper, not farther.

### reading _The Sheltering Sky_

I had never heard of it until I {% vbook_post visited Dublin with my wife | 2025-08-07-ireland-trip %} last year.
In a small bookshop I spoke with the attendant a young man roughly my age about books and when I told him I admired Cormac McCarthy's prose he brought up Paul Bowles most wellknown novel.
A day or two later while perusing charity shops in Dublin I came across a copy.
So I bought it.
And a year on, I read it.
I see no similarity to McCarthy's writing.
In fact, the introduction talks of Bowles's use of [_automatic writing_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automatic_writing), which in my mind only amplifies the distance between these two writers.
I am more inclined (apparently I like that word today) to liken _The Sheltering Sky_ to {% vbook_post _Molloy_ | 2024-12-19-molloy %} and to _Wise Blood_, most obviously for Kit's obsession with omens and irresistability to premonitions.
The key image – the "sheltering sky" that "" – seems to me a gnostic image: an intervening mask hiding a truer, greater, more fearsome power behind.

### playing pitch 'n' putt

An affordable, challenging, and grounding way to enjoy the outdoors, in company of friends or of one's own thoughts.
Who knows – like I said to my friend Da, maybe playing golf well will become a new endeavour of mine.
Its cost and culture might prevent it, however.
The endeavor might be something else: how to foment golf for everyone, including women and working class people.
The problem is the usual one with sharing something one loves: ruining it by overloading it.
The pitch 'n' putt courses are busy enough in the summer.

### enjoying my routine

I'm enjoying my work tasks.
Today I worked until 10am - 6:30pm and felt tempted to return to my desk in the evening.
But I find it impossible to work after dinner.
It just feels wrong.
Gross.

I play soccer three evenings per week.
Sevens on Monday and Wednesday nights, indoor fours on Sunday evening.
I spend time with people I like and play the game I know (and love) best.
Oh, also, it's exercise.
It's invaluable.

I'm trying to schedule enough activities on weekends.
I've noticed Friday nights feel wasted if I don't do something social.
It suffices to have a good friend come over.
One of the best Fridays I had recently consisted of three activities.
First, I went to a free, public [Nathanial Young](https://www.instagram.com/nathanialpov) concert with (the same) friend Da.
Then I went to a political "FUNdraiser" for a municipal city council candidate with my wife and another friend Ro.
Finally, I went with my wife and our friend Ro to "Good Times Vinyl Night" at my favorite neighborhood haunt.
Perfect night.
