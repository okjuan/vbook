### taking a few days off

Yesterday I woke past ten in the morning and got dressed and made a cappuccino in my ceramic takeaway cup I bought {% vbook_post in Japan almost two years ago | 2024-12-26-japan-trip %} and walked in the cold gray drizzling morning to the nearest metro station.

On the train I listened to J Dilla instrumentals and looked out the window as we approached the city centre.

I emerged from underground through a tiered triangular garden where a flock of pigeons pecked at a large handful of seeds strewn in the dirt behind the bushes.

I came to the coffee shop at eleven o'clock and met with a fellow employee who might soon become my peer.

I drank another cappuccino and she drank a breakfast tea with milk and she told me what it was like to work on her team.

Afterwards, we walked down the street to her office building and took the elevator up to the floor where she worked and met her colleague and then went up to the rooftop patio and the amenity rooms below it.

In the lobby I thanked her and shook her hand and said goodbye and head out back into the city.

My friend Matt was waiting for me several blocks away and I met him under an eave and we greeted each other and we talked about my meeting as we walked to where he usually ate his lunch.

We ordered lemongrass chicken bahn mis and sat in the corner of the tiny shop and ate and talked about work and about our friends and about the soccer team we want to start.

Outside we hugged goodbye and he headed back to his office.

I happened across a famous cafe in the city and I stopped inside to take another cappuccino with me on my walk.

I listened to my music and walked over the hill towards the north shore of the downtown peninsula.

I arrived at the pub almost two minutes late but neither team had scored yet.

Individuals and little groups sat apart from each other looking up at the screens mounted on the walls.

I invited myself to sit next to a man in his forties or fifties and told him I liked his jersey.

The striker on the team we all supported took a shot from an unlikely position and when the ball arced over the goalkeeper's outstretched arm and onto the back of the net many of us stood and yelled and shouted and smacked each others palms in celebration.

I ordered a beer and said goodbye to the man at my table and moved to the side of the pub where I could sit in a low chair with a backrest and watch the game on a giant screen.

Again I invited myself to sit next to a lone man and he shrugged and agreed the screen was good but commented that the atmosphere was not so good.

Several times throughout the second half my new sitting partner clapped loudly in encouragement or apprecation of what the players were doing and each time continued his clapping for longer than anyone else.

A player on the opposing team faintly pushed a player on our team in the face and the woman behind me commented to her friend that the opposing player should have been shown a yellow card at least and in fact deserved a red card and that she knew from experience and then the man I sat with turned his head and shook it as he murmured That's not a red card and the woman said nothing in response that I could hear.

After the match I walked back in the direction of a boutique where an old friend of mine had begun working after moving to the city a few months ago and I found him outside on the sidewalk walking towards the pub where I told him I was.

I followed Zach into the women's boutique and we said hello to the workers and I exchanged names with them and then Zach and I talked about the interior design and how beautiful the house in _Beau is Afraid_  was.

In the men's boutique nextdoor there were fitting rooms that were completely paneled with mirrors on the outside that Zach said they made sure to keep clean and on the second floor there was a small room set back from the old brick wall and enclosed by a wall of glass gridded in dark lines as thin as hair and upstairs from that were two two niches enclosed by low walls and at the top was a skylit platform encircled with what Zach said were the most experimental and priciest articles of clothing in the shop.

I accompanied Zach to an Italian cafe and while we waited for his pizza to heat I asked the workers about the soccer jersey on the wall and one of the workers passing behind us stopped to tell us that the founders of the soccer team came to the cafe before each game.

Alone again I stopped by one of my favorite coffee shops for a decaf cappuccino and went downstairs to the shop that used to be occuppied by a bookseller and bookbinder who'd once told me he didn't read much and only worked with books because he had inherited the business.

For each item I looked at the young shop attendant told me about its maker and out of care for her enthusiasm I stayed longer than I intended and perused most the shelves and I asked if this was their shop and they told me yes, it belonged to the young attendant and the other was her mother here today to help.

I walked back up the hill away from the shore to an old bookshop stocked and strewn with shelves and piles of books and asked the old man and his young employee for _Tinkers_ by Paul Harding and the young man looked in the shelf where it ought to be and the old man told him what the cover looked like and went downstairs to find it.

I spoke for a minute with the young man as he looked straight at me and stood several feet away framed in front beside and behind by books so that I felt like I was John Wilson interviewing an unsuspecting subject going about his daily business.

I descended back into the underground and sat on the train facing backwards and listened to my music and watched the city recede back into the skyline.
