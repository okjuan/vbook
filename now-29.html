settling back into routine

Because I work from home and because my job isn’t stressful, quiet weekdays sometimes soothe me more than weekends do. Summer weekends are fun but not so relaxing. On Monday morning I get up, I make a cappuccino, and then I sit at my desk in a secluded part of our home. I listen to jazz and solve problems one at a time. If I need to tend to personal administrative tasks, I do it there as well. When I remember a chore or think of a new one, I type it into my personal notes and return to work. If the laundry needs to be done, I put it in the machine and return to my desk. If the dishwasher needs to be emptied and filled again, I do it after I eat my lunch. If it’s sunny outside, I sit in the patio with my afternoon coffee. And my manager continues to be pleased with my work. That I might commute to a corporate campus to do my job seems ludicrous.

enjoying work

I am, for the first time, in charge of a team that is developing a new feature. I’ve been in charge of feature development before but only when working by myself. This time I’m working with another developer. A team can be comprised of just two people, right?

I’ve been making decisions about how we should organize the work and so far it’s going well. Our start was a bit rough because we tried to do too much in a single pull request. My attention had been distracted by a different, boring project. Once I finished with that other thing, I turned my full attention and energy to this one. I decided we should abandon the big pull request and restart the process with much smaller pieces of work so we could work in parallel. I had to persuade my teammate but that was a few weeks ago and now we’re going full throttle, each preparing multiple changes at once and reviewing each other’s pull requests.

I’m using AI to prepare multiple pull requests in the background while I work. I then review, test, refine, and, if necessary, rework the AI-generated code into final form. I find it very motivating to complete pull requests. My brain releases a lovely chemical when that green Completed message appears.

Sunday 07/19/2026 – Montreal → Vancouver

Foolishly, I booked our return flight on the day of the World Cup final. The game is supposed to end one hour and twenty minutes before our departure time. I’m consider going to the airport early to watch the whole game there. Otherwise I will have to watch it elsewhere and hurry to the airport afterwards. If the game is tied after ninety minutes there’ll be another half hour of play and if it is still tied, penalties. In that case, they’ll be happening while we board the plane. I hope I don’t miss anything special and I regret not being able to watch the game with my friends at home.

visiting one of her best friends Ev and Ev’s boyfriend Vi. I regretted buying so many meals at restaurants. I should’ve bought groceries immediately and gotten over that silly but strangely powerful aversion to using an unfamiliar kitchen.

Travel is expensive for three main reasons: flights, accommodation, and meals. Our flights were covered by my credit card points and our accommodation by the generosity of our friends. If I’d been more diligent about my meals, I could’ve considered our vacation a frugal success.

I love eating great meals at restaurants when I travel, but decent meals and even good ones are often not worth the expense. A good vacation can be one where most meals are modest ones. Recently, when I’ve travelled to cities, I’ve felt shortchanged. I already live in a good city and I could’ve stayed there and made most my meals in the kitchen I know well. What exactly is the point of visiting a different city? Is it only worth it if you see friends you do not otherwise see?

In the last year, I’ve noticed how my mind expands when I travel. I don’t mean because I see cultures and ways of living different from my own. I mean that my thoughts wander very freely and ideas come to me from all directions. Released from usual comforts and narrow routine, the mind floats untethered, nudged easily, more sensitive than usual to suggestion and whim. Susceptible and open.

An open mind is ready to receive influence not just from the outside world and other people, but also from within. The important but not urgent stuff pushed down in daily life starts to rise until it bubbles up into consciousness. Those things that have troubled you but you’ve had no time and space to acknowledge. Those ideas that have been brewing in the back of your mind. Shower thoughts. It’s healthy to make space for this kind of reflection in one’s regular life, but it’s difficult. Travel, for those who are fortunate enough to afford it, offers an extended period for this kind of release.
